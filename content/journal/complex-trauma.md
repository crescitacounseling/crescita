When most people hear the word trauma, they picture a single terrible event: a car crash, an assault, a combat deployment. That kind of trauma is real and serious.

But many people who come to us say something different. "Nothing that bad happened to me." Then they describe a childhood of walking on eggshells, or a parent who was loving one day and frightening the next, or years of feeling invisible.

That's often complex trauma.

## What complex trauma is

Complex trauma comes from repeated or ongoing experiences, usually in relationships where you couldn't easily leave. Often it happens in childhood, when your sense of safety and self was still forming.

It can include:

- Emotional neglect or a parent who wasn't really there
- Living with a parent's addiction, mental illness, or volatility
- Ongoing criticism, shaming, or control
- Physical or sexual abuse
- Growing up in chaos, poverty, or danger
- Being the child who had to take care of everyone

It's not a contest. You don't need to have had it "worse" for it to count.

## How it shows up in adult life

Complex trauma tends to shape the whole system, not just a few memories. Adults with this history often notice:

- A harsh inner critic and deep shame
- Trouble trusting people, or trusting too fast
- People-pleasing and difficulty with boundaries
- Feeling numb, far away, or disconnected from yourself
- Big emotional reactions that seem out of proportion
- A body that never fully relaxes

> If your nervous system grew up expecting danger, it can take a while to believe it's over.

## Why insight isn't enough

Many people with complex trauma are extremely self-aware. They've read the books and can explain their patterns perfectly. And they still react the same way.

That's because complex trauma lives in the body and in the parts of us that learned to survive. Understanding helps, but healing usually needs more.

## How healing works

Good trauma therapy has an order to it:

1. **Stability first.** Building trust, grounding skills, and enough steadiness that you can handle what comes up.
2. **Getting to know your protectors.** [Parts work](/services/parts-work-therapy) helps you understand the sides of you that learned to survive.
3. **Processing.** [EMDR](/services/emdr-therapy) helps older experiences finally settle.
4. **Building a life that fits who you are now.** Relationships, boundaries, rest, joy.

It takes time, often longer than single-incident trauma. That's okay. Slow work holds.
