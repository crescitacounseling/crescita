The first holiday season after someone dies has a way of ambushing people. You might be doing okay in October. Then a song plays in the grocery store, or someone asks who's hosting this year, and the floor drops out.

If that's where you are, here are a few things that tend to help.

## Expect it to be hard, and let that be okay

There's often pressure to keep things normal "for the kids" or "because they would have wanted it." Sometimes that's true and comforting. Sometimes it's just exhausting.

You're allowed to have a strange, quiet, or sad holiday this year. You're also allowed to laugh, enjoy the food, and have a good moment. Neither one is betrayal. Grief and joy can sit at the same table.

## Decide on traditions ahead of time

The hardest moments are often the ones you don't see coming. It can help to talk with your family beforehand about which traditions to keep, which to change, and which to skip this year.

Some families keep everything exactly the same. Some do something completely different, like a trip or a meal out. Some create a new ritual to include the person who's missing:

- Lighting a candle at dinner
- Each person sharing a favorite memory
- Making their signature recipe
- Hanging an ornament that was theirs
- Leaving an empty chair, or deliberately filling it

There's no right answer. The point is choosing on purpose instead of getting blindsided.

## Plan for the hard days

Some days will be heavier than others: the holiday itself, the anniversary, their birthday. Give yourself a plan for those days. Who will you be with? What's one thing that might help, even a little? What can you take off your plate?

> Grief doesn't follow the calendar, but the calendar can still be useful. Knowing a hard day is coming lets you prepare for it.

## Let people help, specifically

People usually want to help and don't know how. "Let me know if you need anything" rarely leads anywhere. If you can, give them something concrete: a meal on a certain night, help with shopping, company on a hard afternoon.

## Watch for grief that gets stuck

Grief is not a problem to fix. But sometimes it gets tangled up with other things. If the death was sudden or traumatic, you might keep replaying the worst moments. If the relationship was complicated, guilt or anger can make grieving harder.

If you're months in and still can't function, or the memories keep intruding, that's a good time to talk with someone.
