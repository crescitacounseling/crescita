May is Mental Health Awareness Month. Expect a lot of advice in your feed: morning routines, gratitude journals, ten-step plans to a better you.

Some of that is helpful. A lot of it is overwhelming, especially if you're already struggling. So here's a shorter list. Small things that tend to matter more than they look.

## Notice how you're actually doing

Most of us are better at tracking our to-do list than our inner state. Once a day, try a quick check-in: How's my body? What am I feeling? What do I need right now?

You don't have to do anything with the answer. Just noticing is a skill, and it gets easier with practice.

## Protect your sleep a little

Not perfect sleep. Just slightly better sleep. A consistent wake-up time, a dimmer screen at night, a phone that charges in another room. Sleep affects mood, anxiety, patience, and focus more than almost anything else.

## Move your body, gently

You don't need a new workout plan. A walk around the neighborhood counts. So does stretching for five minutes or a slow loop at Garden of the Gods. Movement helps your nervous system process stress.

## Talk to one real person

Isolation makes almost everything harder. Text a friend, call a sibling, sit with a neighbor. You don't have to unload everything. Connection itself helps.

> Taking care of your mental health doesn't have to look impressive. It just has to be real.

## Notice the stories you're telling yourself

"I should be over this by now." "Other people have it worse." "I'm too much." These thoughts can quietly keep people from getting support. Try asking: would I say this to a friend?

## Know the signs that it's time for more support

Small steps help, but they aren't a substitute for care when you need it. Consider talking to a therapist if:

- You've felt low, anxious, or numb for more than a few weeks
- It's affecting your work, relationships, or sleep
- You're relying more on alcohol, food, or screens to cope
- Old experiences keep showing up in your reactions
- You just feel stuck and can't figure out why

If you're having thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 any time.

## Starting is often the hardest part

Many people wait years to try therapy. They're not sure it's "bad enough," or they've had an experience that didn't help. You don't need a crisis to start, and you don't need the right words.
