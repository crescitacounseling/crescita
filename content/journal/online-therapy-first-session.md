Starting therapy is a big step, and doing it over video can add a layer of questions. Will it feel awkward? What if my internet cuts out? Where am I supposed to sit?

If you're in Colorado and considering online therapy, here's what your first session tends to look like.

## Before the session

After your free 20-minute call, you'll get access to a secure client portal. That's where intake paperwork lives: basic information, consent forms, a few questions about what's bringing you in. It usually takes 15 to 20 minutes.

Before each session, you'll get a link. You click it at your appointment time. There's no app to figure out and nothing to install on most devices.

One requirement: you need to be physically in Colorado during each session. That's a licensing rule, not ours. Denver, Grand Junction, a cabin outside Buena Vista, your parked car on a lunch break in Pueblo, all fine.

## Setting up your space

You don't need anything fancy. A few things help:

- **Privacy.** A room with a door, or headphones if others are home. Some people do sessions from their car, and that works fine.
- **A steady connection.** Wi-Fi is better than cell data if you have it.
- **Something to sit on that's comfortable.** You might be there for 50 minutes, or 90 for EMDR.
- **Tissues and water nearby.** Just in case.

## The first session itself

The first session is longer than most. It's mainly a conversation. Your therapist will ask about what's going on now, a bit of your history, and what you're hoping might change. You can share as much or as little as you're ready to. You don't need to have the right words.

You'll also have room to ask your own questions: how the therapist works, what therapy might look like over the next few months, what happens if it doesn't feel like a fit.

> Most people are surprised by how quickly the screen fades into the background.

## If the tech acts up

It happens. If the video freezes or drops, just rejoin the link. If it keeps failing, your therapist can switch to a phone call so you don't lose the time.

## Is online therapy right for you?

For most adults and many teens, video therapy works about as well as sitting in the same room. Many people find it easier to open up from their own space. Some prefer the office. Plenty mix both.
