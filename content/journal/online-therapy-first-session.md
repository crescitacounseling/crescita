Your first session is Tuesday at 10. The link is sitting in your inbox, your laptop is charged, and you're wondering whether to sit at the kitchen table or on the bed, and whether it's weird to do this in sweatpants.

It isn't weird. Sweatpants are fine.

If you're looking into online therapy in Colorado, here's the short answer to what you're probably wondering: a first video session looks a lot like a first session in our office on Weber Street. You talk, your therapist listens and asks questions, and by the end you both have a rough sense of what you'd work on. The difference is that you pick the room.

## Before you log on

Everything starts with a free 20-minute phone call. After that you get access to a secure client portal, which is where the intake paperwork lives: contact information, consent forms, a few questions about what's bringing you in. Most people finish it in 15 to 20 minutes. If you're using insurance, keep your card handy.

Before each session you'll get a link to click at your appointment time. No app to learn.

One rule surprises people. You have to be physically in Colorado during every session. That's a licensing requirement, not a Crescita policy. Monument, Woodland Park, a rental in Breckenridge, your desk in Lakewood, your truck in a quiet corner of the King Soopers lot: all fine. A ski weekend in Park City is not, so we'd move that one.

## A ten-minute setup checklist

You don't need a ring light. You need a place where you can talk without editing yourself. Run through this the day before:

- **A door that closes**, or headphones if it doesn't. If someone else is home, a fan running outside the door covers more sound than you'd expect.
- **Your device plugged in.** The first session is longer than the rest (intake runs up to 75 minutes), and a dying battery at minute 60 is a rough way to end.
- **Wi-Fi over cell data** if you have the choice.
- **The camera at eye level.** Stack a couple of books under the laptop. It's easier to feel connected when your therapist isn't looking up at your ceiling fan.
- **Notifications off.** Silence your phone and close the email tab.
- **Water and tissues within reach.** Not because you'll definitely cry. Just so you don't have to get up if you do.
- **A plan for kids and pets.** A show queued up, the dog already walked. Interruptions happen and nobody minds, but fewer is easier.
- **Five minutes of buffer.** Log on early so you aren't sprinting from a work call straight into the hardest conversation of your week.

If your car is the most private place you have, use it. Plenty of parents do sessions from the driveway. Park somewhere you can stay put, and don't plan to drive while we talk.

## What the first online therapy session in Colorado actually covers

The first few minutes are usually the most awkward part, and they pass. We'll make sure the audio works, go over a few basics like confidentiality, and then get into it.

Mostly, it's a conversation. I'll ask what's happening right now, a bit about your history, and what you'd like to be different six months from now. You can share as much or as little as you're ready to. "I honestly don't know where to start" is a perfectly good place to start.

You'll have time for your own questions too. How do you work? What would the next few sessions look like? What if this doesn't feel like a fit? Ask all of it.

> Most people tell me the screen stops registering somewhere around the fifteen-minute mark.

Something people don't expect: talking from your own couch, with your own coffee, can make it easier to open up. For some people that's the whole reason they finally book.

If the tech acts up, it happens. When the video freezes, just rejoin the link. If it keeps failing, we can switch to a phone call so you don't lose the time.

After that first meeting, regular sessions are 50 minutes. EMDR sessions run 90, and yes, EMDR works by video. Instead of following a hand across the room, you might follow a dot on the screen or tap on your own shoulders. I offer it both in the office and online, and the [EMDR therapy page](/services/emdr-therapy) explains how it works.

## Quick answers about video sessions

### Is online therapy as effective as in person?

For most adults and most concerns, yes. Research on video therapy for anxiety, depression, and trauma has been encouraging for years. One of the best predictors of whether therapy helps is the relationship with your therapist, and that carries over to a screen just fine. Plenty of people mix both.

### Do I need to be in Colorado?

Yes, physically, for every session. Our therapists are licensed in Colorado, so that's where you need to be when we meet. You don't need to live in Colorado Springs, though. Clients join from Pueblo, Fort Collins, the Western Slope, and plenty of small towns in between. Our page on [online therapy anywhere in Colorado](/services/online-therapy-colorado) has the details.

### What if I have no private space at home?

Very common, especially for parents, people with roommates, and anyone in a small apartment. A parked car is the most popular fix. Others use a walk-in closet, a back patio when the weather cooperates, or a bookable study room at the library with headphones on. If none of that works, coming into the office is always an option.
