For a lot of kids, summer is pure freedom. For anxious kids, it can feel more like the ground shifting.

School, for all its stress, provides a lot of structure: a clear schedule, familiar faces, predictable expectations. When that disappears, some children get clingy, irritable, or stuck in worry loops. Bedtimes drift. Screens fill the gaps. By July, parents are often wondering why their kid seems more anxious on vacation than during finals.

## Why structure matters for anxious kids

Anxiety feeds on uncertainty. When kids don't know what's coming next, their brains try to fill in the blanks, often with worst-case scenarios.

Routine is a quiet form of reassurance. It tells the nervous system, "You know what happens next. You're okay."

## Ideas that help

**Create a loose daily rhythm.** Not a minute-by-minute schedule. Just a few anchors: a consistent wake-up time, meals at roughly the same times, an afternoon outdoor block, a wind-down routine at night.

**Preview the week.** A simple calendar on the fridge showing what's coming (a camp, a visit, a pool day) can lower anxiety a lot.

**Mix in movement and outdoor time.** Colorado summers are made for it. Hiking, biking, the splash pad, even a walk after dinner helps burn off nervous energy.

**Keep some predictable connection.** A weekly playdate or a standing activity with the same group of kids keeps social muscles active and reduces dread about going back to school.

**Watch the screens, gently.** Screens aren't the enemy, but long unstructured days can turn into hours of scrolling, which tends to leave anxious kids feeling worse. Setting a few screen-free windows helps.

> The goal isn't a perfect summer. It's enough predictability that your child can relax into the free time.

## Don't fill every hour

Overscheduling can backfire too. Anxious kids also need downtime and boredom, which is where creativity and self-soothing grow. Aim for a balance between plans and open space.

## Prepare for the transition back

Two or three weeks before school starts, shift bedtimes and wake-up times back gradually. Visit the school if you can, especially if your child is changing schools. Talk about what's coming and what they're looking forward to.

## When it's more than summer jitters

If your child's anxiety is getting in the way of everyday life, like refusing activities, constant reassurance-seeking, panic, or physical complaints with no medical cause, it may be time to get support.

Summer can actually be a good time to start [child and teen therapy](/services/child-and-teen-therapy), without homework and school pressure in the mix. Kids can build skills before the school year starts.
