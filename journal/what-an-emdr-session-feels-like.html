What an EMDR Session Actually Feels Like

September 24, 2026 · 5 min read · Katie Fortunato, LPC

People who are curious about EMDR usually have the same question, even if they phrase it differently: what am I actually going to be doing in there?

Fair question. EMDR has an unusual name and a reputation for being a little mysterious. Here's what a session tends to look like at our office on North Weber, and what clients tell us it feels like.

Before you ever touch a memory

The first few sessions aren't EMDR processing at all. We talk about what brought you in, what you've already tried, and what your life looks like right now. We also build what we call resources: ways to settle your body when things get stirred up. A calm place you can picture. A person, real or imagined, who makes you feel safe. A breath that actually works for you.

This part can feel slow. It's on purpose. EMDR goes better when your system trusts that it can come back to steady ground.

The processing part

When we start working on a specific memory, we'll ask you to bring up a picture of it, the belief about yourself that comes with it ("I'm not safe," "It was my fault"), and where you feel it in your body. Then we add bilateral stimulation. That might mean following my hand with your eyes, holding small buzzers that alternate left and right, or tapping.

You notice whatever comes up. A different memory. A wave of sadness. Something that seems unrelated. A tight jaw. Every so often we pause, and you tell me briefly what you're noticing. You don't have to explain it or make it make sense.

You don't have to explain it or make it make sense. Noticing is the whole job.

Over a session or several, the memory usually starts to feel further away. It's still yours. It just stops grabbing you the same way.

What it can feel like

Honestly, it varies. Some sessions are emotional. Some feel oddly boring. Many people are surprised by how much shows up in the body: heat, heaviness, a sudden yawn, tears that come without a story attached. All of that is normal, and none of it means you're doing it wrong.

You stay in control the whole time. If you want to stop, we stop.

How a session ends

We never send you out the door raw. The last part of every session is about closing things down: going back to your resources, checking how your body feels, and talking about what to expect over the next few days. Some people feel tired afterward. Some notice vivid dreams. That usually settles quickly.

EMDR sessions at Crescita run 90 minutes, which gives us time to do real work and still leave you grounded before you head back out onto Weber Street.

Is it right for you?

EMDR tends to help with trauma, anxiety, and reactions that feel bigger than the moment calls for. It isn't the right first step for everyone, and that's something we can figure out together on a free 20-minute call.
